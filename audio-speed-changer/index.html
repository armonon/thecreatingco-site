Frequently Asked Questions

Can I change audio speed without changing pitch?

Yes. Modern time-stretching algorithms (like WSOLA and phase vocoder) separate speed from pitch, so you can speed up or slow down audio while the pitch stays exactly the same.

What speed range does the audio speed changer support?

The tool supports 0.25× to 4×. Speeds below 0.5× are useful for transcription; 1.25×–2× for efficient listening; above 2× for skimming content.

Will changing speed affect audio quality?

Minimal changes at 0.5×–2× are virtually transparent. Extreme changes (0.25× or 4×) may introduce slight artifacts, but output remains clear and usable for most purposes.

What file formats are supported?

MP3, WAV, OGG, FLAC, M4A, and AAC are accepted as input. Output is typically MP3 or WAV for maximum compatibility across all devices and platforms.

Is my audio file uploaded to any server?

No. Processing happens entirely in your browser using the Web Audio API and WebAssembly. Your audio never leaves your device — completely private by design.

What is 1.5× speed good for?

1.5× is the sweet spot for consuming lectures, podcasts, and audiobooks efficiently. You save one-third of the time while most speech remains perfectly intelligible.

Can I slow down music to learn it by ear?

Absolutely. Set the speed to 0.5× or 0.75× to hear every note clearly without the pitch dropping. This is the standard technique used by musicians transcribing solos or picking out chord changes.
