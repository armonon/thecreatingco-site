Frequently asked questions

What is an aspect ratio?
An aspect ratio is the proportional relationship between the width and height of an image or video frame. It is expressed as two numbers separated by a colon — 16:9 means 16 units wide for every 9 units tall. It defines the shape of the frame independent of the actual pixel dimensions, so 1920×1080 and 1280×720 are both 16:9.
What aspect ratio should I use for YouTube?
YouTube's standard is 16:9. Common resolutions are 1920×1080 (Full HD), 2560×1440 (2K), and 3840×2160 (4K). For YouTube Shorts, use 9:16 — 1080×1920 is recommended. If you upload a non-16:9 video, YouTube adds black bars (pillarboxing or letterboxing) to fill the player.
What aspect ratio for Instagram Reels and TikTok?
Both use 9:16 vertical. Recommended resolution is 1080×1920. If you film on a smartphone in portrait mode, you are already in 9:16. For Instagram feed posts, 1:1 (square) and 4:5 portrait also work well and take up more space in the feed.
How do I find the height from a width and aspect ratio?
Divide the width by the first number in the ratio, then multiply by the second. For 16:9 at 1920px wide: 1920 ÷ 16 × 9 = 1080px. Enter the width in the calculator above with your ratio and the height is calculated automatically.
How do I calculate an aspect ratio from my image dimensions?
Enter both your width and height and the calculator finds the ratio, simplifies it to its smallest whole-number form (e.g., 1920×1080 → 16:9), and shows the decimal value. This is useful when you have an image from a camera or client and need to know what aspect ratio it is.
What is the difference between 16:9 and 4:3?
16:9 (widescreen) is today's standard for HD and 4K TVs, monitors, and online video. It is wider and shorter relative to its height. 4:3 was the standard for older TVs and early computer screens. It is more square-shaped. Most modern video production uses 16:9 unless shooting for specific formats like iPad video (4:3) or cinema (2.39:1).
What aspect ratio do DSLR cameras shoot in?
Most DSLRs and mirrorless cameras shoot still photos in 3:2 — the classic 35mm film ratio. A 24MP sensor typically produces a 6000×4000 image. Many cameras also offer in-camera crop modes for 4:3, 1:1, and 16:9. Video on most DSLRs is shot in 16:9.
