## Specific offer to test

FOR: owner-operated video/content studios with 2–10 people repeatedly handing footage, versions and delivery files between tools.
PROBLEM hypothesis: repeated manual preparation, missing assets and revision handoffs cost billable time. Vendor case studies corroborate the category, not demand for our service.
PROMISE: identify one measurable recurring workflow bottleneck, then demonstrate a reproducible improvement using one customer-approved sample and their existing tools. No promised percentage/time saving.
PRODUCT: scoped workflow diagnosis, written map, baseline timings, one small reversible automation or template if feasibility is established, and a handoff checklist. It is a service—not a license to unqualified alpha software.
DIFFERENCE hypothesis: founder-led creative-software implementation with portable outputs and explicit recovery, not a forced suite migration.
PROOF: show an actual rights-cleared test before claiming capability or impact; current product code/tests are not customer case studies.
PRICE hypothesis: $500 diagnostic (maximum 4 delivery hours); optional $2,500 fixed-scope implementation (maximum 12 delivery hours) only after discovery and reviewed scope. No recurring contract or payment requested in research.
CTA for approved customer-facing version: discuss one recurring workflow and show one example, without committing to a purchase.
EXCLUSIONS: full editing projects, plugin licensing, proprietary media transfer without consent, regulated/security-critical automations, unlimited revisions, guaranteed ROI and unsupported native products.

## Economics gate (assumptions, not actual costs)

At an illustrative fully loaded delivery rate of $75/hour, a 4-hour $500 diagnostic costs $300 and leaves $200/40% contribution before fees and acquisition. A $2,500 implementation at 12 hours costs $900 and leaves $1,600/64% before other costs. These are sensitivity assumptions, not measured margins. Record actual hours, tool costs, payment fees and support; reject scope exceeding capacity or acceptable contribution. Founder labor is not free.

At a later, unvalidated $10,000 implementation, 100 completed projects/year produce $1M annual project revenue, not MRR. At 40 delivery hours each this requires 4,000 delivery hours plus sales/support, so it is not a solo-founder plan. The $2,500 pilot price instead requires 400 projects/year. Price escalation requires demonstrated scope/value and buyer acceptance. Pilot capacity limit: ONE concurrent client until actual workload is measured. First provisional $1K revenue milestone: two completed $500 diagnostics, subject to actual delivery and payment evidence.

## Non-leading interview (15–20 min)

1. Walk me through your last completed client project, from incoming assets to final handoff.
2. Where did you repeat work, lose files, or wait on someone? Show the last example if comfortable.
3. How often did that happen in the last month? What did you do to fix it?
4. What tools or people do you already pay for? Who controls that budget?
5. What happens if nothing changes? Is there a dated upcoming project affected?
6. What have you tried, and why did it not work?
7. Only after understanding the problem, show the scoped concept. What would prevent using it?
8. Would a written proposal for this specific problem be useful? Who else would need to assess it?

Do not ask leading 'would you pay for AI?' questions. Keep exact customer quotes/contacts in the existing private CRM, not the company dashboard. Aggregate IDs and consent-safe summaries only.
