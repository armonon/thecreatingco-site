# INVENT: Make the second session the magic trick

2026-10-09 · One compact creative cycle · Proposal only; no implementation or release authorization.

## Premise and assumptions

The suite's strongest prospective promise is **“Finish something today. Change your mind tomorrow.”** Demonstrate editable creative intent surviving a real boundary, rather than sell four feature lists. This is a proposed positioning, not a claim competitors cannot do it.

Founder-reported revenue is $0; traffic and retention are unknown. All four full golden workflows remain unqualified. HEALTH.md reports useful scoped evidence, not proof of native readiness or reliable real-world use. We cannot identify the empirically weakest product from these summaries; each candidate deliberately stresses a documented weak boundary. Assume existing owners can supply one rights-cleared, tiny fixture and an exact-source local build. No new paid service, shared architecture, deployment, or replacement owner is needed.

## 1. ENCORE — Your performance has a tomorrow

**Big idea.** A performance is not a disposable recording: yesterday's spontaneous gesture becomes today's editable composition. Make reopening—not recording—the reveal.

**User experience.** In StemDeck, perform a short four-deck passage from supplied sounds: one mute, one fade, one cue change. Capture it, save, and fully quit. Reopen the saved work; replay the original, then change one captured moment in the arrangement and export a genuinely different ending. The final sound can accompany a supplied ScenePilot motion card through ordinary manual audio import. A compact closing image names the suite's four creative surfaces: image, object, motion, performance. Only the demonstrated workflow receives a reliability claim.

**Differentiation / wow.** “You didn't have to perform it again.” The change is audibly consequential, and the restart makes the preservation promise credible. The suite is introduced through a memorable creative philosophy, not a launcher or cross-app integration claim.

**Business value.** Tests a specific audience and repeat-use motive: performers who want to finish and revise captured ideas. A second-session edit is stronger activation evidence than a download. It may later support qualified workflow education or services; no paywall or paid offer is part of this prototype.

**Feasibility / risks.** Scoped WebKit capture, reopen, archive and PCM checks provide a starting point. Actual replay scheduling, real musical behavior, physical/native paths and storage remain unresolved. A synthetic clip may hide the failure; use one real human performance. Existing StemDeck owner selects supported actions and records exclusions before the run. Do not claim universal action reconstruction.

**First prototype.** One local, manually guided “original / tomorrow's ending” session using the existing UI and a tiny rights-cleared sound pack. Capture exact source, environment, original project and independently playable exports. Restart the application process; restore into an empty application state with originals unavailable to expose archive dependencies. Fix the first reproducible preservation/export defect through the existing owner and queue, then repeat that same fixture. The optional ScenePilot card is presentation, not a prerequisite or another implementation project.

**Measurable success test.** Proposed acceptance: three independent cold-start runs preserve every declared event and referenced asset; original replay meets an owner-declared timing tolerance; the edited export contains the intended change and plays in an independent player. For five consenting target testers, record unaided completion, assistance and time; require at least four to explain the performance-to-edit benefit without prompting. These are future thresholds, not observed metrics; technical failure blocks a public reliability claim.

## 2. THE LAST-MINUTE CHANGE — A finished poster that isn't finished

**Big idea.** Make the dreaded “one tiny change” the product demonstration: a beautiful finished image still has its decisions inside it tomorrow.

**User experience.** Open a small Luma event poster, adjust a masked light/color treatment and export. Save, quit and reopen. A brief arrives: “Same treatment, different subject color.” Change the retained adjustment—not the flattened export—and export again. Place the new image into a supplied ScenePilot title sequence by explicit file replacement; show both endings side by side.

**Differentiation / wow.** The visual flourish becomes an editable recipe, not a filter baked into a picture. The viewer sees why image-making and motion belong together without being promised automatic synchronization.

**Business value.** Tests whether recurring promotional variations create a reason to return. Measures revision utility rather than demand for another generic image editor.

**Feasibility / risks.** Layered-browser and adjustment evidence makes a narrow case plausible; masked, styled and partial-opacity semantics and production/source parity still need qualification. Visually plausible output can conceal alpha errors. A source-identified local candidate must not be represented as the current public app.

**First prototype.** One poster with two diagnostic overlapping regions, one mask and one partial-opacity adjustment. Preserve a before-save reference; cold reopen, make the briefed change, and compare independently decoded exports against specified affected and unaffected regions. Route any defect to Luma's current owner. ScenePilot replacement is optional manual demonstration only.

**Measurable success test.** Proposed acceptance: three cold reopens retain the declared structure and adjustment settings; independent output checks meet predeclared pixel/alpha tolerances; four of five target testers make the revision without rebuilding the treatment. Record failures, time and assistance separately.

## 3. THE OBJECT WITH TWO FUTURES — Same sketch, different destination

**Big idea.** A small creative object can remain editable while producing both a usable independent file and a compelling visual identity.

**User experience.** In Form, modify a simple badge from a supplied sketch, save and close. Reopen and change one dimension. Export to a currently supported geometry format and inspect it in an independent viewer. A supplied visual composition shows how that badge could anchor a Luma cover and ScenePilot opening card; label these as prepared examples, not live handoffs.

**Differentiation / wow.** The image has a real editable object behind it. “Looks right in our canvas” is visibly separated from “exists correctly outside our app.”

**Business value.** Tests interest in making coherent objects and presentation assets, potentially useful to small creative brands; actual manufacturing suitability remains outside the claim.

**Feasibility / risks.** Scoped browser save/reopen/export evidence exists, but CAD/Rig history handoffs and native/Fabric paths are unresolved. Independent export may lose units or dimensions. Exclude fabrication and cross-workspace transitions from this first case.

**First prototype.** One sketch-to-object path, one dimensional edit after cold reopen, one supported export and independent inspection. No new renderer, connector or fabrication service.

**Measurable success test.** Proposed acceptance: three cold-start runs retain the editable source; independently read geometry matches declared dimensions, units and topology expectations; four of five testers correctly distinguish the editable project, exported object and prepared visual examples.

## Recommendation and hard boundary

**Choose ENCORE only.** It has the clearest emotional reveal and directly exercises documented replay, preservation and export risks. The recommendation is a hypothesis, not a quality ranking. If exact-source execution is blocked by storage or mandatory checks, preserve the blocker; do not build a workaround platform or quietly switch owners.

Do not build a suite launcher, shared file format, cloud sync, universal undo, collaboration layer, asset marketplace, new app, or subscription system. Do not claim all four products are qualified because one fixture passes. Preserve review, security, hosted-CI and release gates: this local prototype can inform repairs while releases remain blocked.
