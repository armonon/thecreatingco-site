## Session protocol (roughly 20–30 minutes; not measured completion time)

1. Ask about the last real custom-lettering/font job: desired output, destination app, current method, time/problems, and what they actually paid. Do not start by pitching premium features.
2. Ask the participant to make a short word/sample using original artwork. Let them choose it within the supported character set. Observe without steering through every control. Record help separately from unaided completion.
3. Ask them to save a project backup, close/reopen it, export their chosen format, use it in their destination app and reopen the saved destination document. Never close unsaved unrelated work.
4. Record whether the result solves that actual job, what failed, and where they would otherwise finish it. Missing symbols/fallbacks, font install friction and spacing issues count as outcomes, not user mistakes.
5. Only afterward ask: what important additional job remains? Is it repeated? Which workaround do they use, what does it cost, and would they actually request a solution?
6. A price concept is internal research only, for a specific genuinely NEW extension they identified. Explain it is not available to buy and existing free features stay free. The earlier $19 example is arithmetic, not an approved price or evidence of value. Record the exact concept shown; never treat a polite yes as payment.

Directional bar: 4/5 complete save/export/use; 3 independently identify the same unmet additional job; at least 2 request that extension after a concrete internal price concept. These are experiment criteria, not current results. Intent is not a sale.

Stop the journey on data loss or unusable output and fix it. If none of five identifies a meaningful additional need, retain the useful free tool and do not build Pro. After ten qualified attempts without consistent paid need, stop that paid-extension hypothesis. Recruitment failure alone does not prove product rejection.
