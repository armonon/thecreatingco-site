# Organic tool-led growth plan

**Prepared:** 2026-10-09  
**Purpose:** Turn the existing browser tools into useful organic entry points that introduce creators to thecreateco and its connected creative workflows.  
**Status:** Strategy and proposed sequence only. No pages were changed, published, outreach sent, or new spend authorized.

## The strategy

Use focused web tools as the front door, but do not optimize all tools equally or treat SEO traffic as the outcome. Each tool should solve one real, searchable job, deliver a trustworthy result, and offer a natural next step into a related thecreateco workflow. Grow the paths that lead to qualified use and repeat value; keep weak or unready paths useful without manufacturing activity.

The loop is:

**Search or useful content → job-specific page → successful first result → save/export/use → relevant sister workflow → return/share/qualified signup → measured learning.**

Organic traffic, rankings, feature availability, and commercial demand are currently unmeasured or incompletely qualified. Treat the proposed priority order as a test plan, not an ROI forecast.

## Product and content priorities

1. **Glyph Studio — first SEO/activation pilot, gated by current qualification.** Own a clear “make a font from your lettering” landing page and a practical first-font tutorial. Current work has narrow Inkscape evidence, but creator validation, broader destination-app proof, and release gates remain. CTA: make a sample, export it, use it; relevant next step: Luma. Keep alpha limitations explicit.
2. **Split + Key & BPM — audio discovery cluster, gated separately.** Build genuinely useful explanatory pages around separating a rights-cleared track and analyzing key/tempo, then connect successful Split users to StemDeck when the handoff is qualified. Split needs the real quality/device/performance/recovery benchmark; Key & BPM is better treated as a free entry point than an assumed paywall.
3. **Captions / Reframe / AutoCut — one creator-video workflow cluster, not three near-duplicate SEO pages.** Explain distinct jobs and show real input-to-output examples; connect to ScenePilot only where the save/edit/export workflow is verified. Use real footage before making quality or time-saving claims.
4. **Other tools — maintain accurate, indexable product pages; expand only when evidence supports a distinctive user job.** Do not generate thin keyword pages for every feature or start a new app lane to chase search volume.

Priorities can change with measured impressions, useful completions, creator feedback, and product readiness. This plan does not supersede active reliability work, named release gates, or existing product holds.

## Execution sequence

### 1. Establish a trustworthy baseline (first)

- Unblock the existing W02 analytics task through its documented access path; separately establish Search Console/property access and verify which domains/subdomains are in scope. No proxy traffic estimates.
- Define one privacy-respecting funnel across the marketing site and tool origins: landing visits, tool starts, successful outputs, exports/saves, relevant handoffs, consented launch-list signups, and return use where measurable. Use aggregate events and minimal retention; do not collect creative files or personal data just to measure SEO.
- Give forms a real source/campaign field or equivalent attribution that is tested end-to-end. A pageview is not a signup; an impression is not a user; a recorded outcome count is not company traffic.
- Capture a dated starting snapshot by page and query: indexed/canonical status, impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, organic sessions, tool-start and successful-output rates, signup/handoff rates, and performance/accessibility checks. Leave unavailable fields unknown.

### 2. Fix discoverability and site foundations

- Make each public tool page crawlable with a unique title/H1/meta description, concise original explanation, clear browser requirements, truthful limitations, visible demo/output, and a descriptive CTA.
- Verify canonical URLs, robots/noindex rules, sitemap coverage, redirects, page status codes, duplicate metadata, internal links, mobile layout, keyboard accessibility, and mobile/desktop performance. Keep app hosts/subdomains stable unless evidence supports a migration; cross-link them clearly to the company hub.
- Add structured data only when the page visibly supports it (for example, SoftwareApplication or VideoObject); validate output. Do not add FAQ markup solely to chase rich results.
- Link from educational pages to the exact tool and from each tool to one contextually relevant next workflow. Avoid irrelevant “all our tools” link blocks that dilute the user's task.

### 3. Run small intent-led pilots

- Pilot one ready-enough cluster at a time, beginning with Glyph after its destination and onboarding gates clear. Keep the existing five-creator activation test distinct from SEO traffic; it answers different questions.
- For each pilot, write the hypothesis, audience/job, query intent, landing/tool CTA, evidence-backed claims, success measure, cost, window, and stop/iterate/scale rule before publishing.
- Build one strong page and one genuinely helpful tutorial/demo per job, not dozens of interchangeable keyword pages. Demonstrate the actual workflow and disclose alpha caveats.
- Promote useful content through already-authorized company channels and relevant communities only under their rules. No paid search, mass outreach, or new spending is part of this plan.

### 4. Improve activation and connected-workflow growth

- Remove avoidable friction before the first useful result; do not require an account before a free result without a functional reason.
- Make output recovery, export, and the next step obvious. After a successful outcome, show a relevant sister tool based on what the user just did (for example, Glyph → Luma; Split → StemDeck; video utility → ScenePilot), not a generic suite pitch.
- Add a voluntary return/share mechanism only when it genuinely helps the creator (reopenable project, reusable workflow, or export). Do not add dark patterns, forced referrals, or unsupported “save anywhere” promises.
- Treat free tools as trust and acquisition assets. Monetization is a separate decision requiring demonstrated additional value, fulfillment readiness, and real cost-to-serve evidence; preserve existing free functionality.

### 5. Review and reallocate

- Review the funnel weekly using comparable intervals and denominators. Separate search impressions/clicks, qualified tool starts, successful outputs, return use, referrals, consented signups, and revenue.
- **Scale** only when a page repeatedly attracts relevant visitors who complete the job and take a useful next step without quality/privacy issues. **Iterate** when intent is present but activation or handoff leaks. **Stop or maintain** when search demand is weak, traffic is irrelevant, or the underlying workflow is not ready.
- Feed learnings into the existing company queue and marketing experiment records. Do not create a parallel CRM, analytics system, or SEO task queue.

## Measurement tree

- **Reach:** indexed pages, query impressions, organic clicks, non-brand share, qualified landing sessions.
- **Activation:** tool starts / eligible landings; successful outputs / starts; exports or saves / successful outputs.
- **Connection:** relevant sister-tool handoffs / successful outputs; consented signup / eligible sessions; return use / activated users where reliable.
- **Business:** qualified conversations, validated additional-value requests, purchases, refunds, contribution after delivery/support/acquisition cost. Keep unknown until sourced.

Use counts and rates with source, interval, and denominator. SEO position alone is not a company growth metric. Do not set arbitrary traffic goals before a baseline exists.

- Product readiness remains uneven. Publish no unsupported performance, privacy, compatibility, or professional-quality claim. Keep release/PR/CI gates and active product owners intact.
- Do not spend, change prices, make a public major promise, or send mass outreach under this plan. Existing authorized routine marketing policies still apply.
