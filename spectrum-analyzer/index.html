Who uses an audio spectrum analyzer?

From mixing engineers to acoustic researchers — frequency analysis is a universal audio tool.

🎛️ Mixing Engineers
Compare the frequency balance of a mix against a reference track. Identify frequency masking between instruments. Confirm EQ decisions before printing.
🔊 Acoustic Treatment
Use the microphone input to sweep a room and see room modes — the standing waves that cause bass buildup in studio corners and home listening rooms.
🎤 Vocal Producers
Spot sibilance peaks in a recorded vocal. Find the frequency of resonances to notch out before heavy compression makes them worse.
🎸 Instrument Setup
Analyze the output of an amplifier, instrument pickup, or PA speaker to identify tonal issues before the recording session starts.
🎓 Audio Education
Teachers can show students in real time how different instruments, chords, vowels, and consonants look in the frequency domain — a living textbook.
🔬 Research and QA
Test microphones, speakers, and audio interfaces by measuring their frequency response against a reference signal without expensive dedicated hardware.

Frequently asked questions

What is an audio spectrum analyzer?
An audio spectrum analyzer shows the frequency content of a sound in real time — which frequencies are loud and which are quiet. The horizontal axis represents frequency (20 Hz to 20 kHz) and the vertical axis shows the amplitude (loudness) in decibels (dB). Engineers and musicians use it to identify problem frequencies, compare sounds, and make EQ decisions.
Is the online spectrum analyzer free?
Yes — completely free. Open the page, allow microphone access (or upload a file), and the real-time FFT display appears immediately. No account, no download, no time limit.
What does the spectrum analyzer show?
The analyzer shows a real-time FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) plot: the amplitude of each frequency band from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, updated 60 times per second. The display also shows a peak-hold line and a scrolling spectrogram (color waterfall) for tracking how frequencies change over time.
Can I analyze an audio file (MP3, WAV) instead of live mic input?
Yes — upload an MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, or M4A file and the analyzer processes it offline. You can scrub through the file while watching the spectrum update, or export a screenshot of any frame.
What FFT size does the analyzer use?
The default FFT size is 4096 samples, giving 2048 frequency bins across the 0–22 kHz range at 44.1 kHz sample rate. You can switch to 2048 (faster, less frequency resolution) or 8192 (slower, higher resolution) from the settings panel.
Can I use the spectrum analyzer for music production?
Yes — it's ideal for mixing and mastering decisions: comparing two tracks side by side, identifying resonances, checking the low-end in a mix, verifying a noise reduction result, or simply learning how different instruments look in the frequency domain.
Does the analyzer work on mobile?
Yes — the spectrum analyzer is fully mobile-optimised. It works in Chrome on Android and Safari on iPhone. Allow microphone access and the real-time FFT display starts immediately.
