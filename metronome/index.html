Who uses an online metronome

🎸 Guitarists and bassists
Practise scales, chord changes and riffs in strict time — start slow and increase the BPM as muscle memory builds.
🎹 Piano and keyboard players
Work on difficult passages bar by bar at a reduced tempo until they're clean, then gradually raise the BPM to performance speed.
🥁 Drummers
Lock in with a click track to tighten your groove and eliminate rushing or dragging — the foundation of studio-ready drumming.
🎤 Singers and vocalists
Keep time while learning a new song, practise rhythmic phrasing, or match the tempo of a backing track before recording.
🎓 Music students
Use the metronome during grade practice and sight-reading sessions — a consistent tempo is one of the first things an examiner listens for.
🎛️ Producers and beatmakers
Set a project BPM, loop a section, and tap along with the metronome to find the groove before committing to a tempo.

Frequently asked questions

Is the online metronome free?
Yes — StemDeck's metronome is completely free. No account, no download, and no usage limit.
What is a metronome?
A metronome is a device — physical or digital — that produces a steady beat at a set tempo, measured in beats per minute (BPM). Musicians use it to develop consistent timing, learn difficult passages at a slow tempo, and gradually increase speed as they become comfortable.
What BPM range does the online metronome support?
The metronome supports tempos from 20 BPM (very slow, largo) to 300 BPM (extremely fast). Common practice tempos are 60–80 BPM for slow work, 100–120 for medium, and 140+ for fast passages.
What time signatures does it support?
The metronome supports 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8, 7/8 and 12/8. The downbeat is accented so you can always hear the start of the bar.
What is tap tempo?
Tap tempo lets you set the BPM by tapping a button in time with a song or a passage you want to match. The metronome averages your taps to find the closest BPM, so you don't have to calculate it yourself.
Can I use the metronome on mobile?
Yes — the online metronome works in any modern mobile browser. Enable your device's audio permission and tap the start button to begin.
What are common Italian tempo markings and their BPM?
Larghissimo: below 24 BPM. Largo: 24–54. Adagio: 54–68. Andante: 76–108. Moderato: 86–108. Allegro: 120–156. Vivace: 156–176. Presto: 168–200. Prestissimo: above 200 BPM.
