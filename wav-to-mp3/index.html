WAV to MP3 — FAQ

Does converting WAV to MP3 reduce quality?
Yes — MP3 is lossy. Converting WAV (lossless) to MP3 permanently removes audio data through psychoacoustic compression. At 320 kbps, most listeners cannot distinguish MP3 from lossless in a blind test. At 128 kbps, artefacts can be audible on complex musical passages or when using high-quality headphones.
What MP3 bitrate should I use?
320 kbps is near-transparent for most music — the highest quality you can get from MP3. 256 kbps is transparent to most listeners on most tracks. 192 kbps is fine for podcasts, streaming, and general use. Only drop to 128 kbps for speech content or low-bandwidth applications where file size is critical.
Is the converter free and private?
Yes — completely free with no file count or file size limits. Your audio never leaves your device. Conversion runs locally in your browser using the Web Audio API, so there are no server costs or privacy risks.
Can I convert FLAC or AIFF to MP3 as well?
Yes. The converter accepts any audio format the browser's Web Audio API can decode: WAV, FLAC, AIFF, OGG Vorbis, and AAC/M4A. All are decoded to PCM first, then encoded to MP3 at your chosen bitrate.
Will my MP3 keep its ID3 tags?
Yes — title, artist, album, year, and genre tags from the source file are preserved in the output MP3. You can also edit these tags directly in the tool before downloading if you want to update metadata.
Can I batch-convert a whole folder of WAV files?
Yes. Drag and drop multiple files at once — or click the drop zone to select a whole folder. All files are converted at the same bitrate setting and can be downloaded as individual files or bundled into a ZIP.
