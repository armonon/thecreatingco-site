What you can put in a QR code

One generator handles every common QR code type.

🔗
Website URL
Scan to open any URL in a phone browser. The most common use — great for menus, business cards, and marketing materials.
https://yoursite.com
📝
Plain Text
Display a message, address, coupon code, or any text when scanned. Useful when you don't have a URL to link to.
Coupon: SAVE20
📶
Wi-Fi Network
Let guests join your Wi-Fi by scanning — no typing required. Encodes the network name, password, and security type.
WIFI:S:MyNetwork;T:WPA;P:password;
✉️
Email Address
Open a pre-addressed email when scanned. Optionally pre-fill the subject and body. Great for feedback forms and contact pages.
mailto:you@email.com
📞
Phone Number
Scan to dial a number directly. Useful on printed materials where people may want to call without typing the number.
tel:+15551234567
👤
Contact Card (vCard)
Encode a full contact record — name, phone, email, company, website — that scans directly into the phone's contacts app.
BEGIN:VCARD…

Frequently asked questions

Is the QR code generator free?
Yes — completely free. Enter your URL or text, customise the design, and download the QR code as PNG or SVG. No account, no watermark, and the code never expires.
What can I put in a QR code?
You can encode a website URL, plain text, Wi-Fi network credentials (SSID and password), an email address, a phone number, or a vCard contact card that saves directly to a phone's contacts app.
Do the QR codes expire?
No — these are static QR codes. The data is encoded directly into the code, with no redirect URL or external dependency. The code works as long as the destination URL is live, and there is no expiry, subscription, or tracking.
Can I download the QR code as SVG?
Yes — download as SVG for print-ready, infinitely scalable output that stays sharp on business cards, posters, packaging, and any printed material. Download as PNG for digital sharing, email, or screens.
Can I customise the QR code colours?
Yes — you can change the foreground (module) and background colours to match your brand. Keep sufficient contrast between the two colours (dark on light, or light on dark) so camera apps can reliably read the code.
How big should a QR code be for print?
For reliable scanning, a QR code should be at least 2 cm × 2 cm (about 0.8 in × 0.8 in) when printed. The maximum scan distance is roughly 10× the code's side length, so a 3 cm code scans reliably from about 30 cm away. Use SVG export for print to stay sharp at any size.
Can I use these QR codes commercially?
Yes — the QR codes generated here are yours to use however you like, including on commercial materials such as product packaging, menus, business cards, event signage, and marketing assets. No attribution required.
