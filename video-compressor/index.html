Video compression — FAQ

How much can I compress a video?
Typical compression is 50–75% with modern codecs like H.265. High compression can achieve 80–90% reduction but may slightly lower visual quality. Compression depends on original bitrate, resolution, codec, and content (motion-heavy videos compress less than static-camera). Use H.265 for best compression, H.264 for compatibility.
Is video compression free and private?
Yes — completely free with no file count or size limits. Your videos stay on your device. All compression happens locally in your browser using WebCodecs API. Nothing is uploaded to any server, ever.
Will compression reduce video quality?
Not noticeably with medium settings. High compression may reduce visual quality — think YouTube/streaming quality vs. cinema-grade. Audio is preserved at your chosen bitrate. For social media, messaging, and web sharing, high compression is ideal.
Can I compress multiple videos at once?
Yes — select multiple video files and all will be compressed with the same codec/bitrate/resolution settings. Download them individually or as a ZIP archive. Batch compression saves hours on video workflows.
What video formats are supported?
Input: MP4, WebM, MOV, AVI, MKV. Output: MP4 (H.264/H.265), WebM (VP9), MOV. H.265 in MP4 offers the best compression while maintaining quality across modern browsers. Check browser support for your chosen codec.
Can I undo compression if quality is too low?
No — compression is lossy and cannot be reversed. Always keep your original video. If the compressed file quality is unsatisfactory, adjust the codec/bitrate/resolution and compress again. For testing, compress to medium bitrate first, then decide if you need less compression.
