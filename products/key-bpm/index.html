A GOOD PLACE TO BEGIN

One tool. Three starting points.

1 / 3

Prepare a small DJ crate

Compare a handful of tracks before planning transitions.

  1. Import five or six tracks with contrasting tempos.
  2. Review the key, Camelot and BPM estimates for each track.
  3. Export CSV and audition promising transitions by ear.

Check a known track

Learn how an estimate compares with a reference you trust.

  1. Analyze a song whose key and tempo you already know.
  2. Check whether the tempo lands at half or double time.
  3. Treat differences as a reason to listen, not proof that the reference is wrong.

Find a rehearsal tempo

Use an estimate to begin practicing a part.

  1. Import a recording with a clear pulse.
  2. Compare the estimated BPM with a metronome and the actual groove.
  3. Keep a CSV note, then adjust the practice tempo yourself.

What should I know before starting?

One global key estimate does not describe key changes, modal or atonal music. BPM may land at half or double time. Rough results are fallback estimates. The analyzer is fixture-tested, not benchmarked on a labeled music corpus.

How do I keep my work?

Export the CSV before closing the tool. Check the import requirements of any software you want to use it with.

Can I download a native app?

This page opens the public web alpha. There are no public native downloads yet. Check current availability.
