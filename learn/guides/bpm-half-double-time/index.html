Why a BPM finder reports half or double time

Test notes updated October 10, 2026 · by thecreatingco

Open Key & BPMTool overview & limits

If a BPM finder reports half or twice the tempo you expected, check the pulse by ear. A 120 BPM quarter-note beat also has a 60 BPM half-note pulse and 240 eighth notes per minute. Choose the tempo that matches the musical phrasing and your intended grid.

Start with a known-tempo example

Our original 16-second click track contains 32 beat events spaced exactly 0.5 seconds apart, beginning at 0 seconds. That construction gives a 120 BPM pulse. The companion mix adds C-major keyboard tones and a C2 bass at the same timing. Both are mono, 22,050 Hz, 16-bit PCM WAV.

Download the 120 BPM click · Download the C-major test mix · Download our actual Key & BPM CSV

A click track has a known pulse but no useful harmonic key. The mix deliberately contains C, E and G; it is a synthetic tonal exercise, not a benchmark on labelled real songs.

The click was a useful tempo check. The mix did not qualify a supported tempo or key result, despite its known construction. A blank key is an inconclusive result, not C major.

These are observations from a desktop Chrome run on October 10, 2026, using the exact downloads above. They do not measure accuracy across genres. A fixture’s intended key and an estimator’s returned key are separate facts; retain both when comparing results.

Check a half-time or double-time reading

  1. Find a stable section with a clear pulse. Avoid a free-time intro or a breakdown for the first comparison.
  2. Tap or count intervals. Sixteen beat intervals spanning eight seconds give 60 × 16 ÷ 8 = 120 BPM. Counting sixteen events spans only fifteen intervals, so do not confuse events with intervals.
  3. Compare the returned BPM with half and double that number. At 60 BPM you hear every second beat of this fixture; at 240 BPM you are counting subdivisions between its clicks.
  4. Align a grid at the chosen pulse and inspect phrase boundaries later in the track. A correct first beat can still drift if the performance changes tempo.
  5. Export the CSV before leaving the tab and annotate your chosen tempo separately from the raw estimate.

Why key and Camelot results need listening

A global estimate compresses an entire track into one label. A modulation, modal passage, sparse percussion track or shared pitch set can make that label incomplete. Compare the harmonic material where you actually intend to mix; compatible-looking codes do not guarantee that vocals or chord progressions work together.

For the click-only fixture, disregard any key label. For the C-major mixture, listen to the sustained chord and bass instead of treating the filename as independent proof that the detector got it right.

Audacity documents tempo and beats per bar as separate controls in its rhythm-track generator. That distinction is useful when checking pulse against meter. Source: Audacity Rhythm Track manual.

Key & BPM is a public alpha. Its current live page says values are fixture-tested rather than benchmarked on a labelled music corpus. A “Rough” result is a fallback estimate; results remain in the current tab until exported.
