How to check an image export against its editable layers

A flattened picture and an editable document solve different problems. Before closing an image editor, make sure you can keep editing and that the exported image matches the intended layer state.

Updated October 10, 2026 · by thecreatingco

Open LumaApp overview & limits

A small example to work through

Our original poster plan uses three layers: a paper background, a purple circle and a dark title. A fourth reference layer should stay hidden in the final image.

Use the blank observation and decision fields to record your own result. These downloads are planning worksheets; they do not load an app project or demonstrate a completed export.

Try it and inspect the result

  1. In Luma label layers by purpose and choose the intended visible state. Keep a working copy before flattening or exporting.
  2. Export a small test through the available controls. Open the result outside the editor so the preview is independent of the layer workspace.
  3. Compare corners, text and overlapping edges. Keep the editable document alongside the delivery image; a PNG alone cannot preserve a layer stack.

When the result differs

Unexpected sketch in export
Check reference-layer visibility before repeating the export.
Text looks soft
Inspect at the actual delivery size; zoomed previews can conceal resampling.
Background became opaque
Choose whether the delivery needs transparency and verify the resulting pixels, not just the filename.

What this example establishes

The worksheet records an intended composition rather than a tested Luma export. For transparent delivery, use the separate PNG edge guide and inspect several backgrounds before sharing.

This page supplies a worked example and a review method. It does not report a newly completed end-to-end app test. For current availability and edition details, read the Luma overview.
