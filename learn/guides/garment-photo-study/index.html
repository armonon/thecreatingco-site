How to compare front and back garment photographs

A garment study needs consistent views before a 3D approximation is useful. Mismatched pose, distance and cropping can make the same garment look like two different shapes.

Updated October 10, 2026 · by thecreatingco

Open GhostApp overview & limits

A small example to work through

Our fictional jacket brief has a front photograph 1800 pixels high and a back photograph 1500 pixels high. Compare the shoulder-to-hem span rather than forcing the entire images to match.

Try it and inspect the result

  1. Choose clear front and back photographs with permission to use them. Avoid strongly different poses and wide-angle views.
  2. In Ghost compare the visible silhouette from equivalent directions. Keep source photographs beside the approximate 3D study.
  3. Record which features are visible, obscured or absent. Review a saved result before closing the current workspace; keep the original images independently.

When the result differs

Front and back lengths disagree
Compare an actual garment landmark before rescaling the whole photograph.
One sleeve appears wider
Pose and perspective may explain the difference; do not immediately remodel it.
Want a sewing pattern
Photographs do not recover measured pattern pieces, seam allowance or wearable fit.

What this example establishes

The 1.2 ratio aligns two comparison spans in this invented example. It is not a garment size measurement. Ghost produces approximate visual studies; hidden construction and exact fit need a different source of evidence.

This page supplies a worked example and a review method. It does not report a newly completed end-to-end app test. For current availability and edition details, read the Ghost overview.
