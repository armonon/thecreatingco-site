How to write a project post that gets useful feedback

People can give better feedback when they know what you made, your role and the decision you are trying to make. Keep the brief specific enough to answer in one sitting.

Updated October 10, 2026 · by thecreatingco

Open The CircleApp overview & limits

A small example to work through

Our fictional project is a workshop poster. The author designed the layout and is choosing between a large title and a larger product image. The example is not posted to The Circle.

Use the blank observation and decision fields to record your own result. These downloads are planning worksheets; they do not load an app project or demonstrate a completed export.

Try it and inspect the result

  1. Open The Circle and browse examples before composing your own contribution. Use work you may share and describe your actual role.
  2. Choose a focused question and include enough context to answer it. Review profile and project visibility before using account contribution features.
  3. After feedback, keep your own source files and record the decision. A project post or collection does not replace your originals.

When the result differs

Comments are general praise
Ask about one concrete task, such as locating the date.
Readers cannot tell your contribution
Separate your role from collaborator and source credits.
Want to keep drafts restricted
Review actual visibility controls; a less discoverable link alone does not establish privacy.

What this example establishes

The brief is a reusable editorial example, not a submitted post or a promise of community response. Public-alpha account features may require sign-in. This guide does not create an account or publish to anyone.

This page supplies a worked example and a review method. It does not report a newly completed end-to-end app test. For current availability and edition details, read the The Circle overview.
