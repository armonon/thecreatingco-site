How to tune with the online tuner

Takes about 30 seconds. No setup, no download.

1
Allow microphone access
Open the tuner and click Allow when the browser asks for microphone permission. This lets the tuner hear your instrument. The mic input is never recorded or sent anywhere.
2
Select your instrument and tuning
Choose Guitar, Bass, Ukulele, or Chromatic. For guitar, pick your tuning — standard, drop D, open G, DADGAD, and more. Select the string you want to tune.
3
Play a string and watch the needle
Pluck the string and watch the needle. If the note is flat, tune up (turn the peg until the needle centres). If sharp, tune down. When the needle sits at 0 cents, that string is in tune.

Frequently asked questions

Is the online tuner free?
Yes — completely free. Open the page, allow microphone access, and start tuning. No account needed, no app to install.
What instruments can I tune with the online tuner?
The chromatic tuner works with any pitched instrument: guitar (standard, drop D, open G, DADGAD, and other alternate tunings), bass guitar, ukulele, violin, viola, cello, mandolin, banjo, and more. Switch between preset instrument tunings or use chromatic mode to detect any note.
Do I need to install anything?
No — the tuner runs entirely in your browser using the Web Audio API. You just need to allow microphone access when prompted. It works on Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge on desktop and mobile.
How accurate is the online tuner?
The chromatic tuner uses autocorrelation-based pitch detection accurate to ±1 cent. That's more than precise enough for performance and recording. A good microphone or holding the device close to the instrument improves accuracy in noisy environments.
What is standard guitar tuning?
Standard guitar tuning from low to high is E2–A2–D3–G3–B3–E4. The tuner shows which string you're closest to and whether it's sharp or flat, with a needle display to guide you to perfect pitch.
Can I use the tuner on my phone?
Yes — the tuner is fully mobile-optimised. Open it in your phone browser, allow mic access, and tune. The large needle display is easy to read while your hands are on the instrument.
What is A4 reference pitch and can I change it?
A4 reference pitch is the frequency of the A above middle C, standardised at 440 Hz for most Western music. Some orchestras and historical recordings use 432 Hz or 415 Hz. The online tuner lets you set the A4 reference between 432 Hz and 446 Hz to match your ensemble.
