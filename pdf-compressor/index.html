PDF compression — FAQ

How much can I compress a PDF?
Typical compression is 50–80% depending on the original PDF content. High-compression mode achieves 70–90% reduction but may slightly lower image quality. PDFs with many images compress more (up to 90%) than text-heavy PDFs (10–30% reduction). Use medium compression for best quality/size balance.
Is PDF compression free and private?
Yes — completely free with no file count or size limits. Your PDFs stay on your device. All compression happens locally in your browser using advanced JavaScript algorithms. Nothing is uploaded to any server, ever.
Will compression reduce image quality?
Not noticeably with low or medium compression. High compression may slightly reduce image quality — similar to Instagram-quality photos vs. print-quality. Text remains sharp at any compression level. For document sharing and email, high compression is ideal.
Can I compress multiple PDFs at once?
Yes — drop a folder or select multiple PDF files and all will be compressed with the same settings. Download them individually or as a ZIP archive. Batch compression saves hours on large document workflows.
What happens to PDF content during compression?
Text content and form fields are always preserved. You can optionally remove metadata (author, creation date, comments) and embedded multimedia (JavaScript, audio, video) to further shrink file size and protect privacy. The PDF remains fully readable.
Can I undo compression if quality is too low?
No — compression is lossy and cannot be reversed. Always keep your original PDF. If the compressed file is unsatisfactory, adjust the compression level and compress again. For safety, compress to medium first, then decide if you need less compression.
