Frequently asked questions

Is the reverb remover free?
Yes — completely free. No account, no watermark, no time limit. Upload your audio, remove the reverb, and download the result.
What is reverb and how does it affect audio quality?
Reverb is the collection of echoes that build up when sound bounces off walls, floors, and ceilings. In recordings, it makes voices sound distant, muddy, and hard to understand — especially noticeable in bedrooms, bathrooms, and any untreated room. Removing it makes speech sound recorded in a professional studio.
What audio formats does the reverb remover support?
You can upload MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC, OGG, and M4A. The de-reverbed output downloads as WAV or MP3.
Can I remove reverb from a music recording, not just voice?
Yes — the reverb remover works on any audio type: vocals, speech, guitar, piano, drums, and full mixes. Results are best on single-source recordings rather than dense multi-instrument mixes, where some reverb may be desirable for feel.
Is my audio private?
Yes. Reverb removal runs in your browser using local processing. Your audio is never sent to a server — it stays on your device throughout.
How does the reverb remover work technically?
The tool uses spectral subtraction and a learned model of reverb decay to estimate the direct signal and subtract the reverberant tail. You control the strength of reduction (light, medium, heavy) and can preview before downloading.
What's the difference between reverb and echo?
Echo is a distinct, audible repeat of a sound (like shouting in a canyon). Reverb is the smear of many tiny echoes arriving so quickly they blend together, creating the sense of 'room' or 'space'. Both can be removed or reduced with this tool.
