Audio format guide

FormatTypeBest for
MP3 Lossy Streaming, sharing and general playback. 320 kbps is indistinguishable from lossless for most listeners.
WAV Lossless Studio production and DAW projects. Large files but no quality loss — the standard for professional recording.
FLAC Lossless Archiving and audiophile listening. Same quality as WAV but 40–60% smaller. Widely supported by music players.
AAC Lossy Apple devices and iTunes / Apple Music. Better quality than MP3 at the same bit rate — the preferred format for mobile streaming.
OGG Lossy Web and open-source applications. Similar quality to MP3, royalty-free, and supported natively by most browsers.
M4A Lossy / Lossless iTunes purchases and Apple ecosystem. Container format — most M4A files use AAC encoding inside.
AIFF Lossless Apple's uncompressed studio format — equivalent to WAV, preferred in Logic Pro and GarageBand workflows.

Frequently asked questions

Is the audio converter free?
Yes — Split's audio converter is completely free. No account, no download, and no usage limit.
Which audio formats can I convert between?
The converter supports MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC, OGG, M4A, AIFF and WebM. You can convert from any of these to any other supported format.
Is MP3 or WAV better quality?
WAV is uncompressed and lossless — every detail of the original audio is preserved. MP3 uses lossy compression to reduce file size, which removes some audio data. For archiving or professional production, WAV or FLAC is better. For streaming or sharing, MP3 at 320 kbps is indistinguishable from lossless for most listeners.
What is FLAC?
FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec) is a compressed lossless format — it is smaller than WAV but retains all the original audio data. FLAC is widely supported by music players and DAWs and is the preferred archival format for many audiophiles and producers.
Can I convert a WAV to MP3 without losing quality?
Converting WAV to MP3 always involves some quality loss, because MP3 is a lossy codec. At 320 kbps, the difference is imperceptible to most listeners. If you want to convert between uncompressed formats without any loss, convert WAV to FLAC or AIFF instead.
Are my files private?
Yes — Split processes audio in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to a server and are not stored or shared.
