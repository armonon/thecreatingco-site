Noise types and removal effectiveness

What the noise reducer handles well — and what to expect from each type.

Noise type Description Result Tips
AC / electrical hum 50 Hz or 60 Hz buzz from power lines or ground loops Excellent Use the notch filter preset to target exactly 50 or 60 Hz
Fan / HVAC noise Steady broadband noise from cooling fans or air vents Excellent Record 2 seconds of room tone first for best noise profile
Mic / tape hiss High-frequency white noise from analog sources Excellent Keep reduction at 50–70% to preserve air in vocals
Room reverb Echo and reflections in untreated rooms Good Combine with the built-in de-reverb processing
Keyboard / mouse clicks Transient clicks from typing during recording Partial Use the de-click feature; spectral tools work best here
Wind noise Low-frequency rumble from gusts hitting a mic Partial Apply a high-pass filter at 80–100 Hz before denoising
Music bleed / voices Background music or other speakers in the room Partial Non-stationary noise; use stem splitter for better results

Frequently asked questions

Is the noise reducer free to use?
Yes — completely free with no account required. Upload your audio, adjust the noise reduction level, and download the clean version. No time limits, no file size caps beyond what your browser can handle.
What types of noise can it remove?
The noise reducer targets stationary background noise: room hiss, air-conditioning hum, fan noise, electrical hum (50/60 Hz), tape hiss, and microphone self-noise. It works best on consistent background noise rather than sudden transient sounds.
What audio formats are supported?
You can upload MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC, OGG, M4A, and AIFF. Processed audio downloads as WAV (lossless) or MP3.
Will noise reduction affect my voice or music quality?
Aggressive noise reduction can introduce artifacts — a 'watery' sound on speech or loss of high-frequency detail in music. Use moderate settings (40–60% reduction) for best results. The tool includes a before/after preview so you can compare before committing.
Is my audio uploaded to a server?
No. The noise reducer processes your audio locally in your browser using the Web Audio API. Your files are never sent to any external server.
Can I use it on a phone or tablet?
Yes — the tool is mobile-optimised. You can load voice memos, meeting recordings, or music clips directly from your phone's storage.
How long does noise reduction take?
Processing speed depends on the audio length and your device. A 5-minute recording typically processes in 5–15 seconds on a modern laptop. Longer files take proportionally more time but run entirely in the background.
