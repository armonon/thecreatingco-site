What each frequency band controls

A quick-reference guide to EQ bands and their effect on your sound.

Frequency Range What it controls Boost to… Cut to…
32 Hz Sub-bass Deep rumble, felt more than heard Add floor-shaking weight to kick and bass Clean up mud on recordings without subwoofers
64 Hz Bass Fundamental bass guitar & kick drum punch Make bass full and warm Tighten up a boomy room recording
125 Hz Low bass Body of male vocals, bass note warmth Add body and presence to thin recordings Remove boominess in podcasts and voice-overs
250 Hz Low-mid Warmth vs. muddiness zone Add warmth to acoustic instruments Cut to clean up muddy or boxy-sounding audio
500 Hz Mid Nasal frequencies, guitar body Add presence to keys and rhythm guitar Reduce a nasal, "cardboard-box" sound in vocals
1 kHz Upper mid Presence, vocal intelligibility Help lyrics cut through a dense mix Tame a honky or stiff piano tone
2 kHz Presence Attack of snare, consonant clarity Add edge and bite to vocals or guitars Reduce harshness or ear fatigue in a mix
4 kHz High-mid Snare crack, string definition Add snap and definition to drums Soften a harsh, brittle recording
8 kHz High Cymbals, sibilance, hi-hats Add sparkle and openness De-ess harsh S and T sounds in vocals
16 kHz Air Airy shimmer, perceived space Make a mix sound open and professional Remove hiss without touching the rest of the top end

Who needs an online equalizer?

From home studio producers to video creators — EQ improves every kind of audio.

🎙️ Podcasters & Voice-Over Artists
Boost 1–2 kHz for vocal presence, cut 200–300 Hz to reduce room muddiness, apply a gentle high-pass at 80 Hz to remove mic handling noise.
🎵 Music Producers
EQ individual stems before mixing — add bass warmth to a thin synth pad, cut low-mids to un-bury a kick drum, or brighten a dull acoustic guitar recording.
🎬 Video Editors
Fix dialogue recorded on a camera mic. Cut the rumble below 80 Hz, reduce the 300–500 Hz box resonance, and lift 2–4 kHz for clarity before export.
🎧 DJs & Playlist Curators
Level the playing field between tracks with different mastering styles. A subtle bass boost or high-shelf lift unifies a playlist that sounds inconsistent across devices.
🎓 Music Students
Learn what different frequencies actually sound like by boosting and cutting in isolation. The live curve display makes EQ theory tangible without opening Pro Tools.
📻 Content Creators
Polish a voiceover, a talking-head video, or an Instagram reel. Apply the Vocal Clarity preset, fine-tune, and export — in under a minute.

Frequently asked questions

Is the online equalizer free to use?
Yes — completely free with no account needed. Upload your audio, adjust the EQ bands or choose a preset, and download the processed file. No time limits or file-size caps beyond your browser's capacity.
What is an equalizer used for?
An equalizer (EQ) lets you boost or cut the volume of specific frequency ranges in an audio file. You can add bass warmth, reduce harshness in the highs, cut muddy low-mids, or apply genre-specific presets like 'Hip-Hop', 'Acoustic', or 'Podcast Voice'.
What audio formats does the online EQ support?
You can upload MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC, OGG, and M4A. The equalized output downloads as WAV (lossless) or MP3.
How many EQ bands are available?
The online equalizer provides 10 parametric bands ranging from 32 Hz (sub-bass) up to 16 kHz (air). Each band can be boosted or cut by up to ±12 dB, with adjustable Q (bandwidth) for precise or wide-range shaping.
Is my audio private?
Yes. All EQ processing runs in your browser using the Web Audio API. Your audio is never uploaded to any server — it stays entirely on your device.
Can I use EQ presets?
Yes — the tool includes presets for popular use cases: Bass Boost, Vocal Clarity, Podcast Voice, Hip-Hop, Acoustic, Electronic, Rock, and Flat (reset). Each preset sets all 10 bands at once; you can then fine-tune individual bands.
What's the difference between graphic EQ and parametric EQ?
A graphic EQ has fixed frequency bands at preset intervals (like 31, 63, 125 Hz…). A parametric EQ lets you choose the exact center frequency, gain, and Q width for each band. This online equalizer uses a 10-band graphic EQ layout with a frequency response curve display.
