Who converts MP3 to WAV?

Anyone who needs uncompressed audio for professional workflows.

Music Producers & DAW Users
Pro Tools, Logic Pro, Ableton, and FL Studio all work natively with WAV. Import MP3 stems directly as WAV to avoid re-encoding artefacts on export.
Hardware Sampler Owners
MPC, SP-404, AKAI, Roland, and Teenage Engineering devices often require WAV. Convert your MP3 sample library in bulk before loading to your device.
Video Editors
Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, and Final Cut Pro handle WAV more reliably than MP3, especially for sync-critical dialogue and music under picture.
Podcast Editors
When editing multiple guests recorded as MP3s, converting to WAV first avoids generation loss from repeated encode/decode cycles in your editing software.
Game Developers
Unity and Unreal Engine prefer WAV for sound effects. Convert downloaded MP3 effects to WAV for best in-engine performance and quality.
Audio Engineers & Mastering
Mastering studios require lossless input. Convert client-supplied MP3s to WAV for session import, then work in the lossless domain throughout the mastering chain.

MP3 to WAV — FAQ

Does converting MP3 to WAV improve the quality?
No. MP3 is a lossy format — the audio data that was discarded during compression is gone permanently. Converting to WAV produces a larger, uncompressed file, but the audio quality is identical to the source MP3. WAV is useful for professional workflows that require an uncompressed container, not for recovering lost quality.
Why convert MP3 to WAV if quality doesn't improve?
Many pro tools require uncompressed input. DAWs, hardware samplers, video editors, and broadcast playout systems often prefer or require WAV. Converting also avoids generation loss — every time you re-encode a lossy file, quality degrades slightly. Working in WAV inside your DAW then exporting once to MP3 is better than MP3 → edit → MP3 → edit → MP3.
Is the converter really free? Are there limits?
Yes — completely free, no file count limit, no file size limit (within your browser's memory). Your files never leave your device: conversion runs locally using the Web Audio API, so there are no server costs to pass on.
What sample rate should I pick — 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz?
Choose 44.1 kHz for music — this is the CD standard and matches most MP3 sources. Choose 48 kHz if the WAV will sync to video in a video editor or broadcast environment, where 48 kHz is the standard. Don't choose a rate higher than your source: upsampling adds nothing.
What is the difference between 16-bit and 24-bit WAV?
16-bit is CD standard: 96 dB dynamic range, perfect for music distribution and playback. 24-bit gives 144 dB dynamic range — more headroom for editing, mixing, and processing before noise becomes audible. For archival or DAW editing, 24-bit is better. For a simple format-converted file, 16-bit is fine.
Can I convert other formats to WAV, not just MP3?
Yes. The converter accepts any audio format the browser can decode — including MP3, AAC (.m4a), OGG Vorbis, FLAC, and WebM. All are decoded to PCM and re-encoded as 16-bit or 24-bit WAV at your chosen sample rate.
