What can you use text to speech for?

TTS is a versatile tool for creators, students, professionals, and anyone who benefits from hearing content rather than reading it.

📖
Proofread your writing
Hearing your own writing read aloud reveals awkward phrasing, missing words, and rhythm issues that your eyes skip over when reading silently.
🎓
Study and learning
Listen to notes, textbook passages, or flashcard content while commuting or doing other tasks. Hearing reinforces memory differently than reading.
♿
Accessibility
Makes written content accessible to people with dyslexia, visual impairments, or reading fatigue. Listen to articles, emails, or documents at your own pace.
🌐
Language learning
Hear native pronunciation of foreign text. Slow the speed to 0.6× to hear each word clearly. Change voices to hear different accents and speaking styles.
🎙️
Voice-over scripts
Preview how a script sounds before recording. Check pacing at 1.0× or faster speeds to estimate running time. Catch awkward sentences before you record.
🧠
Focus and comprehension
Some people absorb and retain information better by hearing it. Read along with the voice at 1.0× for dual-channel reinforcement of important content.

Frequently asked questions

What is text to speech?
Text to speech (TTS) is technology that converts written text into spoken audio. Modern browsers include a built-in speech synthesis engine (the Web Speech API) that can read text in multiple voices and languages. This tool uses that engine directly — no third-party service is involved.
How many voices are available?
The number of available voices depends on your browser and operating system. macOS and iOS provide Apple's high-quality voices across many languages. Windows provides Microsoft voices. Chrome and Edge may offer additional enhanced voices. This tool automatically lists every voice your browser currently has installed.
Does this work offline?
The tool itself runs offline. However, some voices require an internet connection to stream from your OS's TTS service. Pre-installed system voices (common on macOS and Windows) work offline. If a voice fails to speak, try a different voice from the dropdown — some are more reliable offline than others.
What is the best speed for listening?
1.0× matches natural human speech. For careful listening (language learning, proofreading, complex content): 0.7–0.9×. For review of familiar material: 1.5–2.0×. For learning or accessibility: 0.5–0.7×. Most people find 1.2–1.4× comfortable once they're used to TTS.
Why does the voice sound robotic?
Voice quality varies by platform. Older built-in voices (especially on Windows 7/8 or older Android) sound mechanical. On macOS, select a Siri voice for the most natural sound. On Windows 10/11, Microsoft voices (Aria, Guy, Jenny) are considerably more natural. Try different voices in the dropdown to find the best one on your device.
Is there a character limit?
There is no hard limit in this tool, but very long texts (tens of thousands of characters) may cause browsers to stop mid-sentence or fail silently. For long documents, split them into sections of a few paragraphs and speak each section separately for the most reliable playback.
Is my text sent to any server?
No — all speech synthesis happens locally in your browser using the Web Speech API. Your text is never sent to any external server by this tool. Note that some of your browser's installed voices may stream from your OS provider (Apple, Microsoft, Google) depending on your system settings.
